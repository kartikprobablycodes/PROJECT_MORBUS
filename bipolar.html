Elena's Storm and Starlight

Elena, 31, is an investigative journalist and poet. When hypomania arrives, she feels an electric symphony coursing through her veins: colors appear saturated by 200%, ideas ignite like supernovas, and she requires only three hours of sleep to write groundbreaking exposes. But when the neurochemical tide recedes, it leaves behind an arid, gravity-heavy depression where lifting an arm from bed feels like moving a granite boulder.

“People romanticize the mania and demonize the depression, but the truth is both are terrifyingly lonely when people only love you for your fireworks. When the fire burns out and only cold ashes remain, that is when I need someone to sit in the dark with me without demanding I shine.”

Through psychotherapy, circadian rhythm stability, and medicine, Elena maintains equilibrium. But what healed her most deeply was when her partner and friends stopped treating her emotions as dangerous anomalies and started viewing them as deep ocean currents requiring a steady anchor of unconditional love.
